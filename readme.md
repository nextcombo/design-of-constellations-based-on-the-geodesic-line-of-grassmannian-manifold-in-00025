# Design of constellations based on the geodesic line of Grassmannian manifold in non-coherent MIMO communication: from geometry theory to numerical implementation [with MATLAB code]

Design of constellations for non-coherent MIMO communication based on geodesic lines in Grassmann manifold: from geometry theory to numerical implementation
1. The problem of MIMO communication system has been widely studied in coherent communication scenario. The theoretical boundaries of capacity gain and diversity gain have been fully characterized. However, When the channel state information is not available at both ends of the communication link, Under the so-called non-coherent communication regime, Traditional training sequence schemes based on channel estimation face fundamental constraints of spectrum efficiency loss. Zheng and Tse (2002) pioneering work revealed the essential characteristics of non-coherent MIMO channels under high signal-to-noise ratio: the entity carrying information is not specific signal values. Instead, it forms a linear subspace - the channel's multiplicative fading only applies rotation and scaling to the basis of the subspace. It does not change the subspace itself.
This geometric insight transforms the problem of non-coherent constellation design into a spherical filling problem on Grassmann manifolds. In a paper published in IEEE Transactions on Information Theory in 2009, Systematically expounds the design criteria of constellation based on subspace perturbation analysis. Point out that the Frobenius norm is an appropriate measure to measure the distance between Grassmannian points. Propose greedy construction Three categories of design methods: direct construction and rotation transformation. This paper, on the basis of inheriting this geometric framework, Further explore the use of geodesic lines on Stiefel manifolds as fundamental geometric elements for constellation construction. Generating orthogonal directions in the tangent space by using Weyl-Heisenberg operator basis. Systematically constructing non-coherent unitary space-time constellation of different scales.
II. System Model Considers a frequency flat block fading MIMO channel with M transmit antennas and N receive antennas, each of which has a length of T symbol periods. Assume the transmit signal matrix is X∈C(T×M), satisfying XHX=I_M under an unitary constraint. In the non-coherent framework, both the sender and receiver do not possess prior information about the channel matrix H∈C^(M×N). The received signal can be expressed as:
Y = X·H + √(M/(T·ρ))·W
where W is a additive complex Gaussian white noise matrix, ρ represents the signal-to-noise ratio parameter. The elements of channel matrix H are independently and identically distributed with CN(0,1).
The core feature of this system model lies in the fact that when ρ →∞, the column space of received signal Y constitutes random perturbation to the subspace spanned by transmitted signals. The input signal maximizing mutual information I(X;Y) should satisfy an isotropic distribution—i.e., uniformly distributed over the Stiefel manifold St(T,M) under the unitary equivalence relation, i.e., Grassmann manifold Gr(T,M). This conclusion directly leads to the geometric approach to constellation design: seeking M-dimensional subspace sets that are "far apart" on the Grassmann manifold.
This paper specifically focuses on the case where T=2M, where non-coherent MIMO systems can achieve maximum communication freedom.
Geometry as the geometric basis for constellation construction: As a Riemannian manifold, the Grassmann manifold has a unique shortest curve between any two points—the geodesic. The geodesic is not only an essential object in differential geometry but also corresponds to the smoothest continuous deformation path of the signal subspace in physical terms. The core idea of this paper is to regard the geodesic as the "track" connecting different constellation points, constructing constellations with excellent distance characteristics by selecting appropriate sampling parameters along the geodesic.
3.1 Construction of tangent space basis Given initial point U=[I_M;0_M]T∈St(T,M), its tangent space has dimension M(T-M)=M² (real dimension is 2M²). Using Weyl-Heisenberg operator basis (i.e., product of generalized Pauli matrices Xm·Z^n, where ω=exp(2πi/M) is the unit root), a set of standard orthogonal bases can be constructed for this space. Specifically, for each d×d basis matrix D_k, construct tangent vectors:
Δ_k=[0_M; D_k], Δ_{k+M²}=i·[0_M; D_k]
After normalization by the Riemannian metric, 2M² orthogonal tangential directions are obtained. Then, each direction and its anti-direction are taken, totaling K=4M² geodesic line directions.
3.2 Numerical and analytical calculation of geodesics For general cases, the geodesic can be calculated through the singular value decomposition (SVD) of the tangent vector: [Q, S, V] = svd(ΔU), where γ(t) = U·V·diag(cos(t·s_i))·VH + Q·diag(sin(t·s_i))·VH
When T=2M and the initial point is at the specific structure mentioned above, an analytical closed-form expression for the geodesics can be derived. Let the lower half part of the tangent direction ΔU be denoted as D∈C^(M×M), then:
γ(t) = [cos(t/√M)·I_M; √M·D·sin(t/√M)·I_M]
This analytical formula significantly reduces the computational overhead in subsequent constellation optimizations, while also revealing a linear correspondence between the geodesic parameter t and the Riemannian distance d_g=t.
IV. Five scenarios for constellation construction Based on the sampling of points on the geodesic orbit, this paper implements the five constellation construction schemes proposed in the paper (Case (i) to (v)), covering a series of constellations from the smallest to the largest scale.
4.1 Case (i): A basic constellation with L=2 is constructed by taking the initial point U and the endpoint G₁(t_max) of the first geodesic. This constellation corresponds to a "counterpart pair" on the Grassmann manifold, even though it is the smallest scale, it provides a starting point and reference for constellation construction.
4.2 Case (ii): A medium-sized constellation with L=4 selects four tangential lines with high integral degree and their counterparts, symmetrically sampling around the midpoint. An offset parameter x is introduced to control the sampling position from the midpoint, and optimal sampling points are determined by scanning x∈[0, t_max/2] with the criterion of minimizing union bound. Taking T=4, M=2 as an example, the optimal offset parameter x=1959 (sampling resolution 10000 points).
4.3 Case (iii): The symplectic set of the product L=D is constructed by constructing an adjacency graph based on the integral matrix of the product, and the size of the largest clique (symplectic set) d = D/2 is obtained using a branch-and-bound depth-first search algorithm. A subset of d meridians and their reverse meridians from the symplectic set are selected to form the constellation. This scheme utilizes the concept of cliques in graph theory to ensure good separation between directions of selected meridians.
4.4 Case (iv): For L=2M², a complete symplectic constellation (group sampling) is constructed using all 2M² meridians, but divides them into two groups with different offset parameters x₁ and x₂. The integral sum of products within each group is close to zero, while maintaining non-zero integral sums between groups, thereby maximizing the constellation size while maintaining acceptable minimum distances. Taking T=4, M=2 as an example, this scheme constructs a constellation of L=16.
4.5 Case (v): For L=4M², a maximum size constellation is constructed by sampling at the midpoint of all meridians to form a constellation with a size of 4M². This scheme achieves the maximum possible constellation size within the parameter space. Taking T=8, M=4 as an example, a constellation of L=64 is constructed; for T=16, M=8, the constellation size can reach L=256.
5. Design Guidelines and Distance Metrics: The performance evaluation of constellations hinges on the distance metric between codewords. For non-coherent MIMO systems, the pairwise error probability is determined by the product diversity (DP), which is defined as:
DP(X_i, X_j) = det(I_M − X_iH X_j X_jH X_i) =∏_{k=1}^M sin²(θ_k)
where θ_k is the leading singular value of X_j^H X_i obtained from the singular value decomposition of X_j. The larger the DP, the stronger the distinguishability of two codewords under high SNR.
This paper also realizes three types of distance metrics: the geodesic (Riemann) distance d_g = √(∑θ_k^2), chord distance d_c = √(∑sin^2(θ_k)), and product integral degree DP. Among them, the chord distance is directly related to the rate performance under high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), while the product integral degree determines the asymptotic behavior of error probability.
Utilizing union bound as an optimization criterion, the given N receiving antennas are optimized through searching for the parameter x that minimizes the union bound UB(X).
VI. Performance Assessment
6.1 Achievable Rate Based on Monte Carlo Method, the non-coherent channel mutual information of each constellation is calculated. For a given codebook C and SNR level, the conditional entropy is estimated by numerically accumulating log-sum-exp terms, and then the achievable rate R=(1/T)·(log₂L−E[log₂∑_k exp(Λ_k−Λ_true)]) is calculated. Simulation results show that with the increase of constellation size, the achievable rate tends to saturate in the low SNR region and shows an increasing trend consistent with log₂L in the high SNR region.
6.2 Error Rate Analysis Using the maximum likelihood detector (based on Frobenius norm measurement): select the codeword index l that maximizes∥C_l^H·Y∥_F². The error symbol rate (SER) and error bit rate (BER) are evaluated through Monte Carlo simulations covering the range of SNR from 0dB to 20dB, with each SNR point configured with 1000 to 100000 independent channel realizations.
6.3 Bit Label Design For larger constellations, the mirror symmetry bit labeling strategy is adopted: the first half of the codewords are encoded naturally, and the second half is assigned labels in a mirrored manner to ensure the largest Hamming distance between labeled codewords relative to the geodesic direction.
Seven. Implementation Key Points and Code Architecture
The numerical simulation platform employs modular design. The underlying layer includes geometric foundational function libraries: geodesic computation (numerical/analytic), distance metric calculation (multiplication integral degree/chord distance/geodesic distance), Riemannian metrics and inner products, Weyl-Heisenberg basis generation, etc. The middle layer is the constellation construction layer, generating codebooks for each Case corresponding to different (T,M) parameter combinations and storing them as .mat files. The upper layer is the performance evaluation layer, which calls the constellation files for reachable rate analysis and BER/SER Monte Carlo simulation.
The pedal set search algorithm adopts a branch and bound strategy: in the induced adjacent graph from the multiplication integral degree matrix, it uses depth-first search to find the largest clique. During the search process, upper bound pruning is applied (if the size of the current clique plus the number of remaining candidate nodes is less than the optimal clique size found so far, the search is stopped), effectively reducing exponential complexity.
Eight. Conclusion and Prospects
This paper combines Riemann geometry with MIMO communication theory to realize the entire process of non-coherent space-time constellation design based on Grassmann manifold geodesics. By systematically exploring five constellation construction patterns, a constellation family ranging from L=2 to L=256 has been established, and a complete numerical verification has been completed from geometric modeling, optimization design to performance evaluation.
The innovation of this method lies in transforming the constellation design problem into an optimization problem of sampling parameters on geodesic orbits, making the constellation structure inherently geometrically interpretable; at the same time, utilizing the orthogonal structure provided by the Weyl-Heisenberg basis ensures controllable separation between geodesic directions.
Future work can be extended in the following directions: first, combine geodesic constellations with rotation transformation techniques proposed by Gohary and Davidson to further expand the constellation scale and endowed with bit label structure of collection segmentation; second, explore joint optimization methods for geodesic sampling parameters to break through the current independent sampling strategy; third, extend the analysis to non-full-degree configuration scenarios where T<2M.
